My theses

A Novel Architecture Integrating LLM and Context-Aware Supervisors for Safe Interaction with Social Robots

Master's thesis · MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · 2024

A framework for safely integrating Large Language Models with a social robot, overcoming limitations such as hallucinations and bias and preventing ethical and security risks. Long-term memory is accessed through Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and LLM-based Supervisors manage the interaction flow, so that the robot can communicate fluently, remember past context and user information, and detect and handle dangerous situations. This work was the starting point of my PhD research.

Keywords: Large Language Models, Social robots, Safety, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Long-term memory

Social Robot and e-Health: Aphel as an Assistant for Patients in Hospital Wards

Bachelor's thesis · BSc in Computer Science · Università degli Studi di Bari "Aldo Moro" · 2020

Co-supervised Master's theses

Enhancing Human–Robot Interaction with Personality and Individual Preferences

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · A.Y. 2024/2025

This thesis presents a human–robot verbal interaction framework that combines natural language processing with movement control, facial expressions, and real-time visual perception, enabling a social robot to communicate fluently, respond accurately to environmental questions, and adapt its physical behavior to the context. While large language models (LLMs) integrated into robots excel at providing factual information and executing contextually appropriate actions, they typically avoid expressing opinions, emotions, or preferences, resulting in neutral and impersonal interactions. To address this limitation, this work introduces a personality and preference model that allows each robot to embody a distinct character, such as being more reserved or more extroverted, and to maintain consistent likes, dislikes, and conversational styles. These traits influence both verbal and non-verbal communication, ensuring that the robot's behavior reflects its personality in a coherent and believable manner. By integrating this model into the multimodal interaction framework, the robot becomes a more engaging, relatable, and socially present partner, capable of fostering deeper user connection and more natural long-term interaction.

Keywords: Robot personality, Preferences and opinions, LLM, Social robotics

Active Vision for Cognitive Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · A.Y. 2024/2025

In this thesis, an extension of a human-robot verbal interaction framework is presented through the integration of visual capabilities. The previous framework has proven effective in generating responses to user questions that are unbiased, free from hallucinations, and socially appropriate. It is also able to select suitable robot movements and facial expressions in line with the context of the interaction. However, a major limitation emerges: the system lacks awareness of the environment surroundings beyond what the user provides, what is stored in internal memory, or what is passed at the code level. This work addresses this issue by enhancing the original structure with new modules capable of using real-time environmental information to support both interaction and the understanding of internal state or safety violations. This extension not only brings significant improvements to the quality of interaction, allowing the robot to respond more accurately to questions about its environment, but also enhances its usefulness in everyday tasks involving environmental context, such as object search. Moreover, it transforms the robot into a more active agent, capable of deciding when to use the camera to gather additional information or to start a contact with the user, scanning the environment to identify them when they are not immediately visible.

Keywords: Active vision, Proactive HRI, LLM, Object detection

Dynamic Long-Term Knowledge Graph Management with Reliability-Based Retrieval for Social Robots

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · A.Y. 2024/2025

This study presents an extension of a human-robot verbal interaction framework. The original framework combines verbal communication with gestures and facial expressions to enhance the interaction and provides information relying on a static knowledge base. Although Large Language Models integrated into robots excel at providing factual information, a static knowledge base limits the establishment of long-term social relationships. This limitation is addressed through the development of a dynamic knowledge graph for long-term information storage and organization. In parallel, this work introduces also an algorithm for the computation of a reliability score to establish the trustworthiness of information and a mechanism to handle conflicting data during execution. This extension enhances the overall quality of information retrieval and lays the foundation for the establishment of long-term social relationship between users and robots.

Keywords: Long-term memory, Knowledge graph, Information reliability, Social robotics

An Innovative Architecture for Enhancing Social Robot Abilities by Exploiting LLM Processing

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · A.Y. 2024/2025

The study presented in this thesis suggests a fresh method to enhance human-robot interaction. This method improves human-robot communication by using large language models (LLMs). Apart from facilitating both natural and safe interactions, the created system combines an innovative smart module in charge of robot movement control. This module is essential for guaranteeing that the motions of the robot match the context of the discussion and follow required safety precautions, therefore helping to simplify the interpretation of interactions. Dynamically controlling these movements, LLMs analyze the circumstances and make decisions about whether or not to take particular action. Moreover, a system of facial expressions has been included to improve the robot's capacity for succinct and unambiguous expression of emotions and intents. Using "breathing" motions—designed to create delicate, natural movements that give the interface more fluidity—is another important contribution. Supervisors communicate with each other to prescribe the actions and emotions to be conveyed, thanks to specific prompts that teach the robot its social status, making the robot aware of its physical presence and of its ability to communicate emotions. As a result, the robot feels more lifelike and responsive to human communication dynamics.

Keywords: Embodied LLM, Gesture generation, Facial expressions, Social robotics

Adaptive Multimodal Conversational AI System with Emotion Recognition and Personalized Interaction Strategies

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics · Sapienza University of Rome · 2025

This thesis presents the design, implementation, and evaluation of an Adaptive Multimodal Conversational AI System that integrates real-time emotion recognition with personalized interaction strategies. The system addresses the fundamental limitation of conventional conversational agents: their inability to perceive, understand, and adapt to the emotional states of users during interactions. The proposed architecture employs a three-modality emotion fusion approach combining visual facial expression analysis, audio speech emotion recognition, and textual sentiment analysis using a weighted late fusion mechanism. A key contribution is an Emotional Profiling System based on Exponential Moving Averages, which distinguishes transient emotional states from persistent personality traits. The thesis also introduces a learning-based Strategy Selection Engine implementing the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB1) algorithm to balance exploration of new interaction strategies with exploitation of known effective approaches, supporting twelve action types such as empathy probes, validation, humor, and coping strategy suggestions. Face recognition enables user identification across sessions, a Real-Time Adaptation Tracker monitors engagement within sessions, and a Timeout-Based Re-Engagement Module proactively re-engages the user when silence is detected. Experimental evaluation demonstrates the system's ability to maintain coherent emotionally-aware conversations, adapt strategies based on user feedback, and provide personalized interactions across multiple sessions.

Keywords: Multimodal emotion recognition, Affective computing, Personalization, Conversational AI
